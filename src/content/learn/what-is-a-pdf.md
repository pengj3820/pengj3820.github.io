## A digital sheet of paper

Think of a PDF as a **digital printout**. When you save a Word document, the text reflows depending on the fonts and screen size of whoever opens it. When you save a PDF, every letter, line and image is fixed at an exact position on a page of a fixed size.

That's why PDF became the standard for anything that must look *exactly right*: contracts, invoices, résumés, forms, manuals, boarding passes and printed books.

## A short history

- **1991** – Adobe co-founder John Warnock describes the "Camelot Project": a way to send documents that look the same on any computer.
- **1993** – PDF 1.0 and Acrobat are released.
- **2008** – PDF becomes an open standard, **ISO 32000-1**.
- **2017/2020** – PDF 2.0 (ISO 32000-2) is published and later updated.

## What's inside a PDF?

A PDF file is a container that holds several kinds of objects:

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Page descriptions** | Instructions like "draw this text at this position in this font". |
| **Fonts** | Usually embedded, so text looks right even if your computer lacks the font. |
| **Images** | Photos and scans, usually compressed (JPEG, etc.). |
| **Vector graphics** | Lines, shapes and charts that stay sharp at any zoom. |
| **Metadata** | Title, author, creation date, keywords. |
| **Interactive parts** | Links, bookmarks, form fields, comments, signatures. |

## Why PDFs are hard to edit

Because a PDF stores *positions*, not *paragraphs*, there is often no concept of "a sentence that continues on the next line". Each line (sometimes each word or letter) is placed separately. That's great for accurate display but makes editing and converting back to Word tricky. See [How to edit a PDF](/questions/how-to-edit-a-pdf/).

## Two very different kinds of PDF

Not all PDFs contain real text. A PDF made from a scanner or phone camera is just **pictures of pages**. You can't search or copy text from it until you run [OCR](/learn/what-is-ocr/). Learn the difference in [Scanned vs text PDFs](/learn/scanned-vs-text-pdf/).
